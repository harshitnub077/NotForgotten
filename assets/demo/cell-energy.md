# Energy in the Cell

## ATP, the energy currency

Adenosine triphosphate, or ATP, is the molecule that cells use to store and transfer energy. ATP consists of the base adenine, the sugar ribose, and a chain of three phosphate groups. When the bond to the last phosphate is broken, ATP becomes adenosine diphosphate and releases energy that drives muscle contraction, active transport, and the synthesis of new molecules. A typical cell recycles its entire pool of ATP many times every minute.

## Glycolysis

Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration and takes place in the cytoplasm. It splits one molecule of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate. Glycolysis does not require oxygen, and it produces a net gain of two ATP molecules and two molecules of NADH, an electron carrier. When oxygen is scarce, cells regenerate the NAD+ needed for glycolysis by fermentation, which in human muscle produces lactate.

## The Krebs cycle

The Krebs cycle, also called the citric acid cycle, takes place in the mitochondrial matrix. Before entering the cycle, each pyruvate is converted into acetyl coenzyme A, releasing carbon dioxide. The cycle then oxidises the acetyl groups completely, releasing more carbon dioxide and loading electrons onto the carriers NADH and FADH2. Only a small amount of ATP is made directly in the Krebs cycle; its main job is to harvest high-energy electrons.

## Oxidative phosphorylation

Oxidative phosphorylation is the final stage of cellular respiration and produces most of the cell's ATP. It takes place on the inner mitochondrial membrane. The electron transport chain is a series of protein complexes that pass electrons from NADH and FADH2 down to oxygen, which is the final electron acceptor and combines with protons to form water. The energy released as electrons move along the chain is used to pump protons across the inner membrane, creating a concentration gradient. ATP synthase is an enzyme that lets protons flow back across the membrane and uses that flow to attach phosphate to ADP, a process called chemiosmosis.

## Mitochondria

Mitochondria are the organelles where the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation occur. They have a smooth outer membrane and a highly folded inner membrane whose folds, called cristae, increase the surface area available for the electron transport chain. Mitochondria contain their own circular DNA, which supports the endosymbiotic theory that they descended from free-living bacteria engulfed by an ancestral cell.

## Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It takes place in chloroplasts. Chlorophyll is the green pigment that absorbs mainly blue and red light. The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes, where light energy splits water, releases oxygen, and produces ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle is the set of light-independent reactions in the stroma that uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugar. Photosynthesis and cellular respiration are complementary: the products of one are the raw materials of the other.
